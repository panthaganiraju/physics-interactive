Schrödinger Time-Independent Wave Equation

Step-by-step guided derivation

1

Introduction

The Schrödinger Wave Equation is one of the fundamental equations of quantum mechanics. It describes the wave nature of microscopic particles such as electrons and helps determine their energy and probability of existence.

The Time-Independent Schrödinger Equation is applicable when the potential energy of the particle does not change with time.

2

Derivation of the Schrödinger Equation

Consider a particle moving along the x-axis. The wave function representing the particle is

ψ(x) = 2A sin(2πx/λ)

where

  • A = Amplitude of the wave
  • λ = Wavelength associated with the particle
3

Step 1: First Derivative of the Wave Function

Differentiating ψ(x) with respect to x,

dψ(x)/dx = 2A cos(2πx/λ) × (2π/λ)

4

Step 2: Second Derivative of the Wave Function

Differentiating once again,

d²ψ(x)/dx² = - (4π²/λ²) ψ(x)

5

Step 3: Using the de Broglie Relation

According to the de Broglie hypothesis,

λ = h/mv

Substituting,

d²ψ(x)/dx² = - (4π²m²v²/h²) ψ(x)

6

Step 4: Using the Total Energy Relation

The total energy of a particle is

E = ½mv² + V

where

  • E = Total energy
  • V = Potential energy

Rearranging,

mv² = 2(E − V)

Substituting into the previous equation,

d²ψ(x)/dx² = - [8π²m(E − V)/h²] ψ(x)

7

Step 5: Rearranging the Equation

Moving all the terms to one side,

d²ψ(x)/dx² + [8π²m(E − V)/h²] ψ(x) = 0

This is the one-dimensional Schrödinger Time-Independent Wave Equation .

8

Three-Dimensional Schrödinger Equation

For a particle moving in three dimensions,

d²ψ/dx² + d²ψ/dy² + d²ψ/dz² + [8π²m(E − V)/h²] ψ = 0

Rearranging,

[-h²/(8π²m)] (d²/dx² + d²/dy² + d²/dz²) ψ + Vψ = Eψ

This is the general three-dimensional Time-Independent Schrödinger Equation .

9

Physical Significance

  • The Schrödinger equation describes the wave nature of microscopic particles.
  • Its solution gives the wave function (ψ) of the particle.
  • The square of the wave function (|ψ|²) gives the probability of finding the particle at a particular position.
  • It helps determine the allowed energy levels of atoms, molecules, and solids.
  • It forms the basis for understanding atomic structure, chemical bonding, semiconductor physics, and nanotechnology.

Quick Revision

  • The Schrödinger equation explains the behavior of microscopic particles.
  • It is derived using the wave function, de Broglie hypothesis, and energy relation.
  • The one-dimensional equation is used when the particle moves only along the x-axis.
  • The three-dimensional equation is the general form used in quantum mechanics.
  • The solution of the equation provides the wave function and the allowed energy levels.
