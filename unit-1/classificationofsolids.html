LEARN

Classification of Solids

According to the energy-band picture, solids are broadly classified as conductors, semiconductors and insulators.

Basic idea

The electrical behaviour of a solid depends mainly on the availability of electrons that can acquire energy and move through the material. The relationship between the valence band, conduction band, and forbidden energy gap \(E_g\) provides a simple way to understand this behaviour.

Key idea: The smaller the effective energy separation between occupied and available states, the easier it is for charge carriers to participate in electrical conduction.

Three classes

Conductor

The valence and conduction bands overlap, or the Fermi level crosses an allowed band. Electrons can move readily.

Semiconductor

A relatively small forbidden gap separates the bands. Thermal or optical energy can excite carriers.

Insulator

A large forbidden gap makes excitation into the conduction band difficult under ordinary conditions.

TEMPERATURE

Effect of Temperature

Temperature changes carrier populations differently in conductors and semiconductors.

Semiconductors

As temperature rises, more electrons can acquire enough thermal energy to cross the relatively small bandgap and reach the conduction band. Hence, the number of mobile charge carriers increases and conductivity generally increases.

Thermal energy ↑ → carrier concentration ↑ → conductivity ↑

Conductors

Conductors already possess many mobile carriers. Increasing temperature increases lattice vibrations and electron scattering, so electrical resistance generally increases and conductivity decreases.

Temperature ↑ → lattice scattering ↑ → conductivity ↓

Insulators

The large bandgap makes thermal excitation across the gap difficult at ordinary temperatures, so the number of free carriers remains extremely small.

Remember These Points

Compact examination-oriented summary.

Conductor

Allowed band is partially filled or valence and conduction bands overlap.

Semiconductor

Small finite forbidden gap separates valence and conduction bands.

Insulator

Large forbidden gap separates valence and conduction bands.

Bandgap

Eg = EC − EV.

Temperature

Semiconductor conductivity generally increases with temperature.

Examples

Cu/Al: conductors; Si/Ge: semiconductors; glass/mica: insulators.

Exam focus

Classification by energy bands: Conductors have overlapping or partially filled allowed bands, semiconductors have a small forbidden gap, and insulators have a large forbidden gap between the valence and conduction bands.
