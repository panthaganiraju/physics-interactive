TOPIC 08

Bloch's Theorem

How electrons move through a crystal lattice under a periodic potential — the qualitative foundation of band theory.

LEARN

Bloch's Theorem

Qualitative understanding of electron waves in a periodic crystal potential.

Why do we need Bloch's theorem?

The Sommerfeld free-electron theory successfully explains electrical conductivity in many metals, but it does not explain why some materials behave as insulators or semiconductors. Band theory considers the effect of the regular arrangement of atoms in a crystal on electron motion.

In a crystalline solid, atoms are arranged in a regular pattern called a crystal lattice. Electrons moving through the crystal experience a periodic potential produced by the positively charged ion cores.

The central idea

Bloch's theorem: The wave function of an electron moving in a periodic potential can be written as a plane wave multiplied by a periodic function having the same periodicity as the crystal lattice.
ψk(x) = eikxUk(x)

Think of it as two parts

eikx

Plane-wave part

Represents the travelling-wave character of the electron.

Uk(x)

Periodic part

Repeats with the same periodicity as the crystal lattice.

PERIODIC POTENTIAL

Periodic Crystal Potential

The regular arrangement of ions creates a repeating potential energy landscape.

Fig. 1: A periodic crystal lattice produces a periodic potential for an electron. The potential repeats after every lattice constant a, with minima near the positive ion cores.

Periodic condition

V(x) = V(x + a)
a = lattice constant

The potential repeats after every lattice spacing a. It is generally minimum near positive ion cores and changes periodically between neighbouring atoms.

Schrödinger equation

d²ψ/dx² + (8π²m/h²)[E − V(x)]ψ = 0
BLOCH WAVE

Bloch Wave Function

The mathematical form that captures both electron propagation and crystal periodicity.

Statement

The solution of the Schrödinger wave equation for an electron moving in a periodic potential is a plane wave modulated by a periodic function having the same periodicity as the crystal lattice.

ψk(x) = eikxUk(x)

How the Bloch Wave Looks

The Bloch wave contains a travelling plane-wave factor and a periodic part that follows the lattice.

Fig. 2: A Bloch wave can be viewed as a plane wave modulated by a periodic function with the same periodicity as the crystal lattice.

Periodic function

The function Uk(x) has the periodicity of the lattice:

Uk(x) = Uk(x + Na)

where N is the number of lattice periods considered.

Bloch condition

Using the Bloch wave function,

ψk(x + Na) = ψk(x)eikNa
The wave function changes its phase as the electron is translated through the crystal, while the probability density remains periodic.

What to observe

  1. The ion cores are equally spaced.
  2. The potential repeats after each lattice constant a.
  3. The electron wave extends across the crystal rather than staying around one atom.
  4. Changing k changes the phase/wavelength of the travelling-wave component.
  5. The periodic factor Uk(x) carries the crystal's periodic structure.
PHYSICAL SIGNIFICANCE

Why Bloch's Theorem Matters

It provides the bridge from individual atoms to electronic bands in solids.

  • Electrons in a crystal move under a periodic potential created by the crystal lattice.
  • The electron wave behaves like a plane wave whose amplitude is periodically modulated.
  • The theorem explains why electrons can move through a crystal without being localized around a particular atom.
  • It provides the theoretical foundation for energy-band formation in solids.
  • Bloch's theorem forms the basis for the Kronig–Penney model and modern band theory.

Probability density

|ψk(x)|² = ψk(x)ψk*(x)

Because the plane-wave factor has unit magnitude, the probability density is governed by the periodic part:

|ψk(x)|² = |Uk(x)|²
The probability density repeats with the lattice periodicity. The electron is therefore not confined to a single atom.

Connection to the next topic

Bloch's theorem is the conceptual foundation for the Kronig–Penney model, which uses a periodic potential to explain allowed energy bands and forbidden energy gaps.
