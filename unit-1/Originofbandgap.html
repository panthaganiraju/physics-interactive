TOPIC 12

Origin of Bandgap

Why a forbidden energy gap appears between the valence band and conduction band in a crystalline solid.

LEARN

What is a Bandgap?

The bandgap is the forbidden range of energy between the top of the valence band and the bottom of the conduction band.

Definition

The energy bandgap \(E_g\) is the minimum energy difference between the bottom of the conduction band and the top of the valence band.

Eg = EC − EV
Important: There are no allowed electron states in the ideal forbidden gap between the valence band and conduction band.

Where does the gap come from?

The gap is a consequence of the interaction of electron waves with the periodic crystal lattice. When atoms form a crystal, their atomic levels split into bands. The periodic potential also causes special conditions near Brillouin-zone boundaries, where electron waves are strongly coupled and energy levels separate into allowed and forbidden regions.

ORIGIN

How the Bandgap is Formed

Two complementary pictures help students understand the origin of the forbidden gap.

Picture 1: Splitting of Atomic Levels

Fig. 1: Interaction among atoms splits discrete atomic levels into closely spaced states; in a crystal these form bands separated by a forbidden gap.

Picture 2: Periodic Potential and Wave Interaction

In a periodic crystal, electron waves are scattered by the regularly spaced ion cores. Near certain wave vectors, particularly at a Brillouin-zone boundary, waves travelling in opposite directions couple strongly. This produces a separation between energy branches and opens an energy gap.

Periodic potential → wave coupling → separation of energy branches → forbidden gap

Key idea

The bandgap is therefore not an arbitrary empty space. It is a direct consequence of the periodic crystal potential and interaction of electron waves with the crystal lattice.

WAVE PICTURE

Bandgap at the Brillouin-Zone Boundary

A qualitative wave picture explains why the periodic lattice can split energy states.

Fig. 2: Qualitative band splitting at a Brillouin-zone boundary. The periodic potential removes the crossing and opens a gap between the two branches.

What happens physically?

  • At a zone boundary, electron waves travelling in opposite directions can form standing-wave combinations.
  • The periodic potential makes these combinations have different energies.
  • The degeneracy is lifted and the two energy branches separate.
  • The separation is the origin of a forbidden energy interval.
SIGNIFICANCE

Why the Bandgap Matters

The size of \(E_g\) strongly influences how a material conducts electricity.

Conductor

Valence and conduction bands overlap or there is no effective forbidden gap at the Fermi level.

Semiconductor

Small finite bandgap; thermal or optical energy can promote carriers.

Insulator

Large bandgap; ordinary thermal energy is insufficient to produce many free carriers.

Energy needed for excitation

An electron can move from the valence band to the conduction band when it receives sufficient energy:

Ephoton = hν ≥ Eg

For optical excitation, this relationship is important in LEDs, lasers, photodetectors and solar cells.

Concept chain

Periodic lattice → interaction of electron waves → splitting of energy states → allowed bands → forbidden gap Eg → electrical and optical properties.
