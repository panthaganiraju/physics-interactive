Introduction to Quantum Mechanics

Understand why quantum mechanics was developed, how classical physics differs from the microscopic world, and how wave–particle duality forms one of its foundations.

Why Quantum Mechanics?

Start with the physical ideas that led to the development of quantum mechanics.

What is Quantum Mechanics?

Quantum mechanics is the branch of physics used to describe the behavior and properties of microscopic systems such as electrons, atoms, molecules and nuclei.

At the microscopic scale, several physical phenomena cannot be adequately explained using the laws of classical physics. Quantum mechanics provides a framework for describing these phenomena.

From Classical Physics to Quantum Physics

Classical physics provides an excellent description of many macroscopic systems. However, phenomena observed at atomic and subatomic scales revealed that a different description was necessary.

Planck's Quantum Idea

One of the important foundations of quantum theory was Planck's proposal that electromagnetic radiation is exchanged in discrete packets of energy called quanta.

E = hν
Energy of a quantum of electromagnetic radiation

Here, h is Planck's constant and ν is the frequency of radiation.

Wave–Particle Duality

Electromagnetic radiation shows both wave-like and particle-like behavior. Wave phenomena such as interference and diffraction demonstrate its wave nature, while phenomena such as the photoelectric effect demonstrate particle-like behavior.

The recognition of this dual nature of radiation was an important step toward the development of quantum mechanics.

Wave Nature

  • A wave is spread over a region of space.
  • It is characterized by wavelength, frequency, amplitude and phase.
  • Interference and diffraction are examples of wave behavior.

Particle Nature

  • A particle has mass and momentum.
  • It carries energy.
  • A particle can be associated with a definite position in a classical description.

Why This Matters

The idea of wave–particle duality leads naturally to the next topic: the de Broglie hypothesis, according to which moving material particles are also associated with waves.

KEY CONCEPTS

Classical vs Quantum Description

Compare the two descriptions before moving to matter waves.

Classical Physics

  • Mainly describes macroscopic systems.
  • Position and momentum are treated as measurable quantities.
  • Energy can often vary continuously.
  • Particle and wave descriptions are traditionally treated separately.

Quantum Mechanics

  • Describes microscopic systems.
  • Particles are represented using quantum states and wave functions.
  • Physical quantities can have quantized values.
  • Wave–particle duality becomes important.
