de Broglie Hypothesis

Understand matter waves, derive the de Broglie wavelength, explore how wavelength changes with momentum, kinetic energy, accelerating potential and temperature.

LEARN

What is the de Broglie Hypothesis?

From wave–particle duality to matter waves.

Basic Idea

Light shows both wave-like and particle-like behavior. In 1924, Louis de Broglie extended this idea to moving material particles. He proposed that a moving microscopic particle is associated with a wave called a matter wave or de Broglie wave.

λ = h / mv = h / p
de Broglie wavelength

Meaning of the Symbols

λde Broglie wavelength
hPlanck's constant
mMass of particle
vVelocity of particle

Since momentum is p = mv, the relation can also be written as λ = h/p.

Why is this important?

  • It assigns a wave nature to moving material particles.
  • It connects particle momentum with wavelength.
  • It provides a foundation for wave mechanics.
  • It explains why wave effects are important for microscopic particles.
DERIVATION

Derivation of de Broglie Wavelength

The mathematical sequence follows the prescribed GCET/AEP Unit-I derivation. Each step appears and then pauses so you can explain it before pressing Play again.

Click Play to highlight Step 1
STEP 1

According to Planck's quantum theory

The energy of a photon is

E = hν = hc/λ    .......... (1)

Here h is Planck's constant, ν is frequency, c is the speed of light and λ is wavelength.

STEP 2

Einstein's mass–energy relation

According to Einstein,

E = mc²    .......... (2)
STEP 3

Equating equations (1) and (2)

hc/λ = mc²
h/λ = mc
λ = h/mc
STEP 4

Apply the idea to a material particle

For a material particle moving with velocity v, the prescribed derivation replaces c by v:

λ = h/mv
STEP 5

Use the momentum relation

Since

p = mv

Therefore,

λ = h/p

This is the expression for the de Broglie wavelength.

APPLICATION 1

de Broglie Wavelength in Terms of Kinetic Energy

The kinetic energy of a particle is

E = ½mv²

Since

p = mv

Squaring both sides,

p² = m²v²

From the kinetic energy equation,

m²v² = 2mE

Therefore,

p = √(2mE)
APPLICATION 1 — CONTINUED

Substitute momentum into the de Broglie equation

λ = h / √(2mE)
APPLICATION 2

de Broglie Wavelength in Terms of Accelerating Potential

When a charged particle is accelerated through a potential difference V, it gains kinetic energy equal to

E = eV

Substituting into the previous relation,

λ = h / √(2meV)
APPLICATION 2 — ELECTRON

For electrons

λ = 12.26 / √V Å
APPLICATION 3

de Broglie Wavelength in Terms of Temperature

For a particle in thermal equilibrium,

E = (3/2)kBT

Substituting into the de Broglie equation gives

λ = h / √(3mkBT)

CHARACTERISTICS

Characteristics of de Broglie Waves

Important points for understanding and examination revision.

1. Inversely proportional to mass

For a fixed velocity, λ decreases as particle mass increases.

2. Inversely proportional to velocity

For a fixed mass, λ decreases as velocity increases.

3. Not electromagnetic waves

Matter waves are associated with material particles and are not electromagnetic radiation.

4. Associated with motion

The prescribed treatment associates matter waves with a moving particle.

5. Rest case

In the ideal relation λ = h/mv, as v approaches zero, λ tends toward infinity.

6. Independent of charge

The basic de Broglie relation depends on momentum, not directly on particle charge.

7. Momentum dependence

The most general form is λ = h/p.

8. Microscopic significance

Matter-wave effects become especially important when the wavelength is comparable with relevant dimensions.
