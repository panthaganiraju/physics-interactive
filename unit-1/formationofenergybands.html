Formation of Energy Bands

How discrete atomic energy levels broaden and form allowed energy bands when a large number of atoms come together to form a solid.

LEARN

Formation of Energy Bands

In an isolated atom, electrons occupy discrete energy levels. When many atoms are brought close together, these levels split into a very large number of closely spaced levels that appear as energy bands.

From isolated atoms to a solid

Consider a single isolated atom. Its electrons can occupy only certain discrete energy levels. If a second identical atom is brought close to it, the interaction between the atoms causes each level to split. As more atoms are added, the splitting becomes larger in number but the separation between adjacent levels becomes very small.

Central idea: N interacting atoms produce approximately N closely spaced energy states from each atomic level. For a macroscopic solid, these states form practically continuous allowed energy bands.

Why do the levels split?

  • Atoms in a crystal are not completely isolated.
  • The electron wave functions of neighbouring atoms overlap.
  • The interaction between neighbouring atoms removes the exact degeneracy of the isolated atomic levels.
  • Each original atomic level therefore splits into many slightly different energy levels.

BAND STRUCTURE

Allowed Bands and Forbidden Gaps

The splitting of atomic levels produces groups of allowed energies separated by forbidden regions.

Fig. 2: Energy-band structure showing the forbidden energy gap Eg between the conduction band and valence band.

Why is this important?

The conduction band lies above the valence band, with the forbidden energy gap Eg between them. The size of this gap is important in classifying solids according to their electrical behaviour. The next topic, origin of bandgap, will explain how the forbidden gap arises and why its size differs among materials.

SIGNIFICANCE

Physical Significance

Energy-band formation is the bridge between atomic physics and the electrical behaviour of solids.

  • It explains why the energy spectrum of a solid is not simply a collection of isolated atomic levels.
  • It provides the basis for the concept of valence and conduction bands.
  • It leads naturally to the concept of a forbidden energy gap.
  • It forms the foundation for classifying materials as conductors, semiconductors and insulators.
  • It is essential for understanding semiconductor devices, LEDs, lasers, solar cells and electronic materials.

Concept chain

Isolated atoms → atomic energy levels → interaction between atoms → level splitting → very closely spaced levels → energy bands → forbidden gaps → electrical classification.

Compact examination-oriented summary.

Isolated atom

Has discrete energy levels.

Interaction

Neighbouring atoms interact through overlapping electron wave functions.

Splitting

Each atomic level splits into many closely spaced levels.

Energy band

Forbidden gap

Energy interval with no allowed electron states.
