Understand what the wave function ψ means physically, why ψ itself is not directly observable, and how |ψ|² gives the probability density of finding a particle.

LEARN

Physical Significance of Wave Function (Born's Interpretation)

Born's interpretation of the wave function.

The wave function, represented by the symbol ψ (psi), is a mathematical function used to describe the quantum state of a microscopic particle such as an electron.

The wave function itself has no direct physical meaning. It is generally a complex quantity and cannot be measured experimentally.

In 1926, Max Born explained that the square of the wave function gives the probability of finding a particle at a particular position in space.

Probability Density

The quantity

|ψ|² = ψ*ψ

is called the probability density.

It represents the probability of finding the particle at a particular point in space.

  • Higher value of |ψ|² → Greater probability of finding the particle.
  • Lower value of |ψ|² → Smaller probability of finding the particle.
  • |ψ|² = 0 → The particle cannot be found at that position.

Probability of Finding a Particle

The probability of finding a particle within a small volume element dx dy dz is

Probability = ψ*ψ dx dy dz

where

  • ψ* = Complex conjugate of the wave function
  • ψ = Wave function
  • dx dy dz = Small volume element

Normalization Condition

Since a particle must exist somewhere in space, the total probability of finding it must always be equal to 1.

This condition is called the normalization condition.

∫ ψ*ψ dx dy dz = 1

A wave function satisfying this condition is called a normalized wave function.

Normalization in Three Dimensions

∫∫∫ |ψ(x, y, z)|² dx dy dz = 1

This equation represents the probability of finding the particle anywhere in three-dimensional space.

Normalization in Two Dimensions

∫∫ |ψ(x, y)|² dx dy = 1

This equation is used when the particle is confined to the x–y plane.

Normalization in One Dimension

∫ |ψ(x)|² dx = 1

This equation is used when the particle is confined to move only along the x-axis.

Important Points

  • The wave function describes the quantum state of a particle.
  • The wave function itself has no direct physical meaning.
  • The quantity |ψ|² represents the probability density.
  • The total probability of finding the particle in all space is always equal to 1.
  • A wave function satisfying this condition is called a normalized wave function.
  • The wave function cannot be measured directly, but its probability density can be used to predict the location of a particle.

Limitations (Conditions) of a Wave Function

For a wave function to be an acceptable solution of Schrödinger's wave equation, it must satisfy the following conditions:

  1. The wave function must be finite at every point in space.
  2. The wave function must be single-valued; that is, it should have only one value at any given point.
  3. The wave function must be continuous everywhere except where the potential energy is infinite.
  4. The first derivative of the wave function must also be continuous and finite.
  5. The wave function should approach zero at the boundaries (vanish at infinity).

Important observation

  • ψ may be positive, negative or complex.
  • |ψ|² is real and non-negative.
  • Large |ψ|² means a greater probability density.
  • Small |ψ|² means a smaller probability density.
  • At a node, ψ = 0 and therefore |ψ|² = 0.

Physical Significance & Conditions

What ψ means according to Born's interpretation and the conditions required for an acceptable wave function.

Meaning of ψ and |ψ|²

ψ — Wave Function

The wave function is a mathematical function used to describe the quantum state. It has no direct physical meaning and may generally be complex.

|ψ|² — Probability Density

The quantity |ψ|² = ψ*ψ represents the probability density of finding the particle at a particular position.

Conditions of an Acceptable Wave Function

  1. The wave function must be finite at every point in space.
  2. The wave function must be single-valued.
  3. The wave function must be continuous everywhere except where the potential energy is infinite.
  4. The first derivative of the wave function must also be continuous and finite.
  5. The wave function should approach zero at the boundaries (vanish at infinity).

Normalization

Because a particle must exist somewhere in space, the total probability of finding it must be one.

∫ |ψ|² dτ = 1

A wave function satisfying this condition is called a normalized wave function.
