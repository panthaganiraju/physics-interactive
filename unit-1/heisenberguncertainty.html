Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle

Understand why the position and momentum of a microscopic particle cannot both be known with unlimited accuracy, and explore the uncertainty relation interactively.

LEARN

Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle

The fundamental limit on simultaneous position and momentum measurements.

Introduction

The Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle was proposed by the German physicist Werner Heisenberg in 1927.

According to the de Broglie hypothesis, an electron exhibits both particle and wave nature. Because of this dual nature, it is impossible to determine the exact position and exact momentum of an electron at the same time.

Statement

It is impossible to measure both the exact position and the exact momentum of a microscopic particle simultaneously with unlimited accuracy.

Mathematical Expression

Δx × Δp ≥ h / 4π
Position–momentum uncertainty relation
ΔxUncertainty in position
ΔpUncertainty in momentum
hPlanck's constant
4πNumerical factor

Energy–Time Uncertainty Relation

Similarly, the uncertainty between energy and time is given by

ΔE × Δt ≥ h / 4π
Energy–time uncertainty relation

Here ΔE is the uncertainty in energy and Δt is the uncertainty in time.

APPLICATIONS

Physical Significance and Applications

The consequences of uncertainty in microscopic systems.

Physical Significance

  • The uncertainty principle is significant mainly for microscopic particles such as electrons, protons and atoms.
  • For macroscopic objects, the uncertainties are extremely small and can usually be neglected.
  • The uncertainty arises from the wave nature of matter and is a fundamental property of nature.
  • It is not caused by defects in measuring instruments.

1. Electrons in the nucleus

The uncertainty principle helps explain why electrons cannot simply be treated as confined classical particles inside the nucleus.

2. Stability of microscopic systems

Quantum uncertainty contributes to the behavior and stability of microscopic bound systems.

3. Binding energy

The uncertainty principle can be used to estimate characteristic energy scales associated with confinement.

4. Bohr orbit estimation

The uncertainty relation can be used to obtain an estimate of the characteristic size of an atomic orbit.

Important distinction

The uncertainty principle does not mean that instruments are simply too inaccurate. It describes a fundamental limit built into the quantum description of microscopic particles.
