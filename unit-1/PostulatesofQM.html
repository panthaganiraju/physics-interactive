Postulates of Quantum Mechanics

The basic framework connecting the quantum state, operators and measurable physical quantities.

Why do we need postulates?

Classical mechanics describes particles using quantities such as position, momentum and energy. Quantum mechanics uses a different mathematical framework. A physical state is represented by a wave function, while measurable physical quantities are represented by operators.

Core idea: A quantum state is described by ψ, and an observable is represented by a corresponding operator.

Basic framework

1

State

The state of a system is represented by a wave function ψ.

2

Observable

Each measurable physical quantity is associated with an operator.

3

Measurement

Possible measured values are related to eigenvalues of the operator.

4

Expectation

The average value is obtained from the expectation value.

Operators in Quantum Mechanics

Mathematical rules representing physical observables.

Definition

An operator is a mathematical rule that acts on a function and produces another function. In quantum mechanics, measurable physical quantities are represented by suitable operators.

Operators corresponding to observables are represented by Hermitian operators, so their measurable eigenvalues are real.

Important operator rules

  • Operators act on wave functions.
  • The order of operations can matter for non-commuting operators.
  • Observable operators are Hermitian.
  • Measurement results are connected with eigenvalues.
EIGENVALUES & EIGENFUNCTIONS

Eigenvalues and Eigenfunctions

The central equation connecting an operator with a possible measurement value.

Eigenvalue equation

If an operator acting on a special function gives the same function multiplied by a constant, that function is an eigenfunction and the constant is its eigenvalue.

Physical meaning

  • Â represents the observable.
  • ψ is a state with a definite value of that observable when it is an eigenfunction.
  • a is the corresponding possible measured value.
When an observable is measured, the possible results are the eigenvalues of its corresponding operator.

Example: momentum eigenfunction

Consider the plane wave

ψ(x) = Aeikx

Using the momentum operator

p̂ₓ = −iℏ d/dx

we obtain

p̂ₓψ = −iℏ(d/dx)(Aeikx) = ℏkψ
Hence ψ = Aeikx is an eigenfunction of p̂ₓ and ℏk is its eigenvalue.
