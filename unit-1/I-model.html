Kronig–Penney Model

A qualitative model of electrons in a periodic crystal potential and the origin of allowed energy bands and forbidden energy gaps.

LEARN

Kronig–Penney Model

A qualitative model used to understand how the periodic arrangement of atoms produces energy bands in a crystal.

Why is the model needed?

In a crystal, atoms are arranged periodically. Therefore an electron does not move in free space; it moves through a periodic potential produced by the ion cores. The Kronig–Penney model replaces the complicated crystal potential with a simple repeating one-dimensional potential.

Main idea: periodic potential + Schrödinger equation → allowed energies separated by forbidden energy regions.

What the model explains

Allowed bands

Ranges of energy for which electron wave solutions can exist.

Forbidden gaps

Energy ranges in which no propagating electron state is allowed.

Connection with Bloch's theorem

The Kronig–Penney model uses a periodic potential, so its wave functions obey the Bloch form:

ψk(x) = eikxUk(x)

This makes the model a natural continuation of the previous topic.

MODEL

Periodic Potential Model

The complicated crystal potential is approximated by a simple periodic potential.

Fig. 1: Idealized periodic rectangular potential used in the Kronig–Penney model.

Qualitative assumptions

  • The crystal is represented as a one-dimensional periodic structure.
  • Each unit cell contains a low-potential region (well) and a high-potential region (barrier).
  • The same potential pattern repeats throughout the crystal.
  • The electron is treated quantum mechanically using Schrödinger's equation.

Important idea

V(x + d) = V(x)

where d is the period of the model potential. The exact notation for the cell dimensions can vary; the essential requirement is periodicity.

DERIVATION

Kronig–Penney Model — Derivation

The derivation is presented in the same sequence as the AEP GCET Unit-I material, while keeping the qualitative nature of the topic clear.

Assumptions of the Kronig–Penney Model

  • The crystal consists of a large number of atoms arranged periodically.
  • The electron moves in a one-dimensional periodic potential.
  • The periodic potential is represented by a series of rectangular potential wells and barriers.
  • The width of each potential well is a.
  • The width of each potential barrier is b.
  • The height of each potential barrier is V0.

Periodic Potential

Fig. 1: Rectangular periodic potential used in the Kronig–Penney model.

The potential energy is

V(x) = 0   for   0 < x < a
V(x) = V0   for   −b < x < 0

Step 1: Schrödinger Equation Inside the Potential Well

Inside the potential well, V = 0. The time-independent Schrödinger equation becomes

d²ψ/dx² + (2mE/ℏ²)ψ = 0

Step 2: Schrödinger Equation Inside the Potential Barrier

Inside the potential barrier, V = V0. Therefore

d²ψ/dx² + [2m(E − V0)/ℏ²]ψ = 0

Step 3: Introducing α and β

For simplicity, define

α² = 2mE/ℏ²
β² = 2m(V0 − E)/ℏ²

The two equations therefore become

d²ψ/dx² + α²ψ = 0    for   0 < x < a
d²ψ/dx² − β²ψ = 0    for   −b < x < 0

Step 4: Applying Bloch's Theorem

Because the potential is periodic, the wave function must satisfy the Bloch condition. Applying Bloch's theorem to the solutions in the well and barrier regions, and matching the wave function and its derivative at the boundaries, gives the fundamental Kronig–Penney relation.

P (sin αa / αa) + cos αa = cos ka

where P is the scattering power of the potential barrier, k is the wave vector, a is the width of the potential well, and α is the wave number inside the well.

Step 5: Condition for Allowed Energy Bands

Since

−1 ≤ cos ka ≤ +1

the left-hand side of the Kronig–Penney equation must also lie between −1 and +1.

Fig. 2: Modified high-quality version of the Kronig–Penney allowed/forbidden-region graph. The blue strip marks the permitted range −1 to +1.

Allowed: If P(sin αa/αa) + cos αa lies between −1 and +1, the corresponding energy values are allowed.
Forbidden: If the left-hand side lies outside the range −1 to +1, the corresponding energy values are forbidden.

Step 6: Formation of Allowed and Forbidden Bands

Hence, the electron can possess only certain energy values. These permitted ranges form allowed energy bands, separated by forbidden energy gaps.

Allowed band ↔ −1 ≤ P(sin αa/αa) + cos αa ≤ +1
This is the central result of the Kronig–Penney model: a periodic crystal potential converts the possible electron energies into alternate allowed and forbidden regions.

Main Conclusions

  1. The energy spectrum consists of alternate allowed bands and forbidden bands.
  2. Electrons can exist only in the allowed energy bands.
  3. Forbidden bands contain no permitted electron energy levels.
  4. As the electron energy increases, the allowed bands become broader, while the forbidden bands become narrower.
  5. The band structure depends on the strength of the periodic potential.

Special Case 1: Tight-Binding Limit (P → ∞)

When the scattering power is very large,

sin αa = 0
αa = nπ

The energy becomes

E = n²h² / 8ma²

Fig. 3: In the tight-binding limit, very strong barriers effectively confine electrons to individual wells, producing discrete energy levels.

The electron behaves as if it is confined inside an individual potential well, giving rise to discrete energy levels.

Special Case 2: Free Electron Limit (P → 0)

When the scattering power becomes negligible,

cos αa = cos ka
α = k

The corresponding energy is

E = ℏ²k² / 2m

Fig. 4: In the free-electron limit, the E–k relation is a continuous parabola and the forbidden gaps disappear.

All energy values are allowed, and the electron behaves like a free electron.

Derivation at a Glance

Periodic rectangular potential → Schrödinger equation in well and barrier → introduce α and β → apply Bloch theorem and boundary conditions → Kronig–Penney equation → −1 ≤ cos ka ≤ 1 → allowed and forbidden energy bands.

Allowed Energy Bands

The most important qualitative result of the Kronig–Penney model is the appearance of allowed bands separated by forbidden gaps.

Fig. 2: Qualitative E–k diagram illustrating allowed energy bands and forbidden gaps.

What does the diagram mean?

  • Allowed band: electron states exist and the Schrödinger equation has acceptable solutions.
  • Forbidden gap: no allowed electron state exists for that energy range.
  • The periodic crystal potential changes the free-electron energy spectrum into a band structure.
  • Band gaps are especially important for understanding semiconductors and insulators.

SIGNIFICANCE

Physical Significance

Why the Kronig–Penney model is important in engineering physics.

  • It gives a simple picture of electron motion in a periodic crystal potential.
  • It demonstrates that electron energies are grouped into allowed energy bands.
  • It explains the existence of forbidden energy gaps.
  • It provides a qualitative foundation for classifying solids as conductors, semiconductors and insulators.
  • It connects the microscopic periodic lattice with macroscopic electrical properties.
  • It prepares the way for the concepts of effective mass, band gap and semiconductor physics.

From Kronig–Penney to band theory

Periodic lattice → periodic potential → Schrödinger equation → allowed and forbidden energies → energy bands → electrical properties of solids.
