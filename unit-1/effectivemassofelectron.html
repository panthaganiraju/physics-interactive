How the curvature of the E–k diagram determines the effective mass of an electron moving through a crystal.

LEARN

Effective Mass of Electron

In a crystal, an electron does not behave exactly like a free electron. The periodic potential of the lattice changes its response to an applied force.

What is effective mass?

The effective mass of an electron is the mass that an electron appears to have when it moves through a crystal under the influence of the periodic crystal potential.

Important: Effective mass is not a change in the actual rest mass of the electron. It is a convenient quantity that describes how the electron responds to an external force inside a solid.

Why does it arise?

For a free electron, the energy-wave-vector relation is parabolic:

E = ℏ²k² / 2m

In a crystal, the periodic potential modifies this relation. The resulting E–k curve may have different curvature in different parts of the band.

The key relationship

1/m* = (1/ℏ²)(d²E/dk²)

The curvature of the E–k curve therefore determines the effective mass.

DERIVATION

Derivation of Effective Mass

The derivation connects electron dynamics, crystal momentum and the curvature of the E–k relationship.

Step 1: Electron velocity in a crystal

For an electron wave packet in a crystal, the group velocity is

v = (1/ℏ)(dE/dk)

where E is the electron energy and k is the wave vector.

Step 2: Apply an external force

The crystal momentum of the electron is

p = ℏk

Therefore, the applied force is

F = dp/dt = ℏ(dk/dt)

Step 3: Find the acceleration

Starting from

v = (1/ℏ)(dE/dk)

differentiate with respect to time:

a = dv/dt = (1/ℏ)(d²E/dk²)(dk/dt)

Step 4: Compare with Newton's law

For a particle of effective mass m*, Newton's second law gives

F = m*a

Using the expressions for force and acceleration:

ℏ(dk/dt) = m*(1/ℏ)(d²E/dk²)(dk/dt)

Step 5: Expression for Effective Mass

Canceling the common factor \(dk/dt\),

m* = ℏ² / (d²E/dk²)
Therefore, the effective mass is inversely proportional to the curvature of the E–k curve.

Special cases from the E–k curve

d²E/dk² > 0

Positive curvature → positive effective mass.

d²E/dk² < 0

Negative curvature → negative electron effective mass.

At a point of zero curvature: d²E/dk² = 0 → m* becomes very large.

SIGNIFICANCE

Physical Significance

Effective mass allows the complicated effect of the crystal lattice to be represented in a simple dynamical form.

  • It describes how an electron accelerates when an external force is applied inside a crystal.
  • It depends on the curvature of the energy band, so it is generally different at different points in the band.
  • Electrons near the bottom of a conduction band generally have positive effective mass.
  • Near the top of a valence band, the electron curvature is negative; this leads naturally to the hole concept.
  • Effective mass is important in semiconductor devices, carrier transport and optoelectronic materials.

Connection to band theory

Kronig–Penney model → energy bands → E–k relation → curvature of E(k) → effective mass → carrier transport.
